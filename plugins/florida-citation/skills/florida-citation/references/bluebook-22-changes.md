# The Bluebook's 22nd edition: changes that matter to practitioners

Rule 9.800(p) sends everything Rule 9.800 doesn't cover to "the latest edition of The Bluebook," now the 22nd (2025). This skill checks those forms through the Indigo Book (`indigo-practitioner.md`), which tracks an earlier edition. This file lists what the 22nd edition changed, so the agent can tell where the Indigo Book is out of date.

- **Our words, not the Bluebook's.** No Bluebook text is quoted. Each item is a fact about the rules, taken from the publisher's own summary and from law-library and bar-journal descriptions, with its Bluebook rule number as those sources give it.
- **Corroborated** means two independent sources agree. **Single source** means one; confirm it against the Bluebook before relying on it.
  - HeinOnline's post restates the publisher's preface, and BYU's post follows the University of Washington guide, so neither counts as independent of those.
- **Scope:** practitioner forms (Bluepages, signals, short forms, case names, courts, statutes, internet sources). Law-review typeface rules are left out, as they are in this skill.
- **Researched** October 2, 2026. The Bluebook's own rule pages need a subscription and weren't read.

## What this skill does differently because of these

- **County in case names:** `build` writes `Cnty.`, the Bluebook's form, not the Indigo Book's preferred `Cty.` A Florida county court's parenthetical stays `Cty. Ct.`, Rule 9.800(c)(2)'s own form. `check` flags neither form in a case name. See [County](#county).
- **September:** Rule 9.800's own examples write `Sept.` (for example 9.800(m)(2), `(11th Cir. Sept. 23, 2014)`). For Florida and federal case citations Rule 9.800 governs, so `check` reports `Sep.` in a date parenthetical with the fix `Sept.`. In a citation outside Rule 9.800, the 22nd edition's `Sep.` is correct.
- **Everything else** below is for the agent to apply when building or reviewing a citation that Rule 9.800 doesn't cover.

## Changes

### Signals and parentheticals

1. **A new signal, *contrast*.** *Compare* is now limited to authorities that are alike in a relevant way; *contrast* introduces authorities that differ. B1.2, R1.2(b). Corroborated (PREF, UW, WSBA, TYPE, DAVIS, RICH). *Indigo Book:* only *Compare ... with ...* (R4.7).
2. **`(citation modified)`.** A parenthetical saying the writer removed internal quotation marks, brackets, ellipses, internal citations, or footnote numbers, or changed capitalization without brackets. The Bluepages advise against `(cleaned up)`. B5.3. Corroborated (PREF, UW, WSBA, TYPE, MARTIN, RICH). *Indigo Book:* discusses `(cleaned up)` favorably; has no `(citation modified)`. In Florida courts, `(cleaned up)` is common in opinions; follow the court's practice and the filer's preference.
3. **Explanatory parentheticals are now required** after *see also*, *cf.*, and *see generally*, and whenever an authority's relevance isn't clear (before, they were recommended). R1.2, R1.5(a). Single source (UW). *Indigo Book:* recommended, and required only with *cf.* and *but cf.*
4. **Nested parentheticals** get explicit guidance, and the order of parentheticals drops "degree-granting institution." R1.5(b). Single source (UW).
5. **`see, e.g.,`:** the comma between the signals is italicized (or underscored) along with them. B1.2. Single source (UW). *Indigo Book:* the comma after *e.g.* isn't italicized.

### Pinpoints and short forms

6. **Pinpoints for every authority** except those introduced by *see generally*. R1.2(f). Single source in effect (UW; BYU follows it). *Indigo Book:* pinpoint when supporting a specific point (R11.7).
7. **Supra and hereinafter:** legislative debates join the materials for which they aren't used; among legislative materials only hearings may use them. B4, R4.2. Single source (UW). *Indigo Book:* R6.2.3 already excludes most legislative materials except hearings.

### Case names and courts

8. **County.** See [County](#county) below.
9. **Long case names (Bluepages):** a clearer rule on abbreviating them, with a precise list of words that may be abbreviated in a textual sentence. B10.1. Single source (UW, which doesn't give the list). *Indigo Book:* R11.4's eight words.
10. **New and changed Table T6 words** (the common-word table, the Indigo Book's T11), among them `A.I.`, `Disp.`, `Ineq.`, `Nat'y`, `Psych.`, `Telecomm.`, `Wkly.` Single source for the list (DAVIS); MARTIN confirms only that many words were added, and still lists Digital as `Dig.` where DAVIS gives `Digit.` `build` uses the Indigo Book's table and doesn't include these.
11. **Table T7 courts:** adds the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (`F.I.S.C.`) and its Court of Review (`F.I.S.C.R.`). Corroborated (UW, DAVIS).
12. **Old reporter names** such as M'Culloch take a turned comma rather than an apostrophe. R10.2.1(a). Single source in effect (UW; BYU follows it).
13. **Lexis cites may use `LX`,** parallel to `WL`. R10.8. Single source in effect (UW; BYU follows it). Rule 9.800 writes LEXIS cites in full (`2010 Fla. LEXIS 62`); `check` doesn't recognize `LX`, so it would be listed as unrecognized.
14. **Subsequent history phrases (Table T8, the Indigo Book's T14):** `rev'd per curiam` and `denying cert. to` removed; `rev'd summarily` and `mandate held` added. Single source (UW). *Indigo Book:* still lists the removed ones.

### Court documents

15. **Electronic docket numbers:** the Bluepages now call for "any electronic docket number," not only a federal ECF number, which covers state e-filing systems and `Dkt. No.` forms. B17.1. Single source (UW). *Indigo Book:* ECF only (R24.4).
16. **Court and litigation documents (R10.8.3)** were reworded, including for multidistrict litigation. Single source (PREF; HEIN repeats it).

### Statutes, rules, and secondary sources

17. **A statute's codification parenthetical** may be left out when the statute isn't cited for its current application. R12.4(f). Corroborated (PREF, UW).
18. **Statutes cited from a database** name the publisher, as in `(West, Westlaw through ...)`. R12.5(a). Corroborated (UW, TYPE). For Florida Statutes, Rule 9.800(f) and (g) govern.
19. **No amendment date** is needed when the subsection cited wasn't amended. R12.7.3. Single source (UW).
20. **American Law Institute** is now `A.L.I.`, not `Am. L. Inst.`, in Restatement and model-code citations. R12.9.4. Corroborated (UW, TYPE; BYU too). *Indigo Book:* `Am. L. Inst.`
21. **State administrative materials** get their own subsection, and later ones are renumbered. R14.4, B14. Corroborated (PREF, UW). For Florida agency materials, Rule 9.800(d) and (h) govern.
22. **Authors:** list up to three; use *et al.* only for four or more. R15.1(a), B15.1. Single source in effect (UW; BYU follows it). Pen names get a rule (R15.1(d), corroborated by PREF and UW), and Wright & Miller and Moore's get special forms (R15.8(c); the details from UW only).

### New subjects

23. **AI-generated content:** a new rule for large-language-model output, AI search results, and AI-generated media. It requires keeping a saved copy of the output and naming the model or platform and the prompt. R18.3. Corroborated (PREF, UW, WSBA, TYPE). *Indigo Book:* no rule.
24. **Archiving internet sources** is now required (a permanent link or a saved copy on file), not just encouraged. R18.2.1(d). Corroborated (UW, TYPE, RICH, BYU), though whether the Bluepages impose it on court filings wasn't confirmed. *Indigo Book:* optional (R33).
25. **Web citations in the Bluepages:** last-updated times in 24-hour form with a time zone; `last visited` for undated pages; site titles abbreviated by the tables. B18.1, R18.2.2(c). Single source (UW).
26. **Tribal Nations:** a new Rule 22 and B22, with an online-only list of federally recognized Tribal Nations (T1.5). Corroborated (PREF, UW, WSBA, TYPE, DAVIS, RICH).
27. **Archival sources:** a new Rule 23 and B23. Corroborated (PREF, UW).

### Tables of abbreviations

28. **September is `Sep.`,** not `Sept.` (Table T12, the months). Two sources: UW, and a law professor's public post seen only as a search-result summary. Treat it as single source. See [the note above](#what-this-skill-does-differently-because-of-these) for how `check` treats it.
29. **Geographical terms (T10):** substantially revised (PREF). The details are single source (UW): new city abbreviations (among them `Aus.`, `Denv.`, `Jack.`, `Nash.`), Houston as `Hou.`, D.C. moved to the territories, `Czech.` and `N. Maced.` for countries.
30. **Typing:** two spaces after a period only in monospaced fonts (R2.3), and block quotations single-spaced and fully justified (R5.1(a)(i)). Single source (UW). The Indigo Book's R9.2 already describes block quotations that way.

## County

- **The 22nd edition uses `Cnty.`** for County in case names. Corroborated by:
  - HLR, one of the Bluebook's four compiling journals, writing *Seven Cnty. Infrastructure Coal.* in a November 2025 note;
  - MARTIN's 2026 table.

  No source reports a change to County in the 22nd edition, and DAVIS's list of new or changed T6 words doesn't include it.
- **History:** `Cnty.` in the 19th edition (2010); `Cty.` in the 20th (2015); back to `Cnty.` in the 21st (2020).
  - The 2015 change: Susie Salmon, "New Blue," *Arizona Attorney* (Sept. 2015); Peter W. Martin, *Citing Legally* blog (Oct. 6, 2015).
  - The return in the 21st: the *Texas Law Review*'s Greenbook public-comment page (Apr. 30, 2025), proposing `Cnty.` to match the Bluebook.
  - Martin reported that Westlaw and Lexis adopted `Cty.` after 2015, which is why both forms turn up in briefs.
- **The Indigo Book** (2023 update): Table T11 gives County as `Cnty.`, with a note that it recommends the traditional `Cty.` For court names, Table T9 gives `<Name> Cnty. Ct.`, and allows `Cty. Ct.`
- **Rule 9.800(c)(2)** writes a county court as `(Miami-Dade Cty. Ct. Oct. 8, 2014)`. For Florida county courts, Rule 9.800 comes first, so `Cty. Ct.` stays there.
- **So the skill:**
  - writes `Cnty.` in case names (the latest Bluebook, through 9.800(p));
  - writes `Cty. Ct.` in a Florida county court's parenthetical (the rule's own form);
  - flags neither form in a case name, since briefs and opinions use both.
