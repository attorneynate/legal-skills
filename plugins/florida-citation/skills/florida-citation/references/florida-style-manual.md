# The Florida Style Manual: the third tier

Rule 9.800(p) sets the order of authority. Rule 9.800 comes first, then the latest edition of the Bluebook, and then, "for citations not covered in this rule or in The Bluebook," the latest edition of the **Florida Style Manual**.

- **Where:** <https://www.floridastylemanual.com/>, free online.
- **Publisher:** the *Florida State University Law Review*.
- **Edition:** the ninth (2024). Its home page says it conforms to Rule 9.800 as effective January 1, 2024 and to the 21st edition of the Bluebook. So it predates the 2025 and 2026 amendments to Rule 9.800 and the 22nd edition of the Bluebook. Where they differ, Rule 9.800 (`fl_cite.py rule`) governs. (Its "How to Use This Manual" page still names the 20th edition.)
- **Not bundled:** the site states a copyright and no reuse terms. This file only describes what the Manual covers and links to it; it copies none of its forms. Don't reproduce its text; send the user to the page.

Checked October 2, 2026.

## When to use it

`check` lists three kinds of citation as unrecognized with the tier "Florida Style Manual": legislative staff and bill analyses, Florida bills (`CS/HB 7027`), and executive orders. Other Florida materials that belong to this tier, which the script doesn't recognize at all:

- fiscal notes and committee materials;
- House and Senate journals;
- vetoes, and reports of legislative offices;
- proposed constitutional amendments;
- court administrative orders.

For these, tell the user to check the form against the Manual's section, and don't guess one. If you can read the Manual's page, follow it and cite the section you used.

## What it covers, by section

Section numbers as the site lists them:

1. **Capitalization:** of particular persons and things, headings and titles, acts, bills, and constitutions, and whether to write "Court" or "court."
2. **Abbreviations:** in text, and in footnotes and citations.
3. **Legislative materials:** the Florida House and Senate journals; bill-information publications; committees, subcommittees, joint committees, and House councils; **legislative staff analyses and fiscal notes**; official legislative reports; recordings and transcripts of legislative proceedings; the Office of Economic and Demographic Research and OPPAGA; legislative rules; and how to identify legislators.
4. **Bills:** introduced and passed bills, citation and reference forms, proposed committee bills and committee substitutes, appropriations bills, and bill construction.
5. **Florida Statutes:** chapters and sections, in text and standing alone, the year, historical references, a statute's name, and quoting statutes. Rule 9.800(f) governs the citation forms; the Manual adds guidance on textual references.
6. **Florida session laws:** the Laws of Florida, chapter numbers, sections of acts, short forms, and parentheticals showing a statutory change. Rule 9.800(i) governs the basic form.
7. **Florida constitutional materials:** in text and standing alone, annotations, and **proposed constitutional amendments**. Rule 9.800(e) governs the basic form.
8. **Executive branch materials:** **executive orders**, executive vetoes, executive departments, Attorney General opinions, the Florida Administrative Code, agency orders and decisions, the Florida Administrative Register, and the Florida Administrative Practice Manual. Rule 9.800(d), (h), and (k) govern the forms they cover.
9. **Judicial branch materials:** rules, published reports, unpublished minutes, **administrative orders**, identifying judges, and The Florida Bar Journal.
10. **Florida cases:** reporters, unpublished opinions, courts, citation forms, and cases in text. Rule 9.800(a) to (c) govern.
11. **Internet citations:** page and site titles, dates, archiving, and the basic form.
12. **Miscellaneous:** correspondence, interviews, press releases, locations, Florida newspapers, and Florida law school publications.
13. **Abbreviation tables:** legislative committees, legislation, state agencies and commissions, rules, common citation abbreviations, Florida law journals, and Florida newspapers.

Sections 14 and 15 reproduce Rule 9.800 and give the Manual's history. Use this skill's bundled copy of the rule (`references/rule-9.800.md`), which is current through the September 1, 2026 amendment.
